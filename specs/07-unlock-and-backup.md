## 1. Goal
The user can unlock Do with a one-time Google Play purchase, and can export, restore and automatically back up their reminders to a file they chose, with no account and no ads. A "Reminders Not Backed Up" warning, copied from Due's Tools menu, appears on the Spec 6 health screen and badge until a user-controlled backup has succeeded.

## 3. Scope

### A. Unlock

**Product.** Agreed direction, already in the brief and in `DO-RESEARCH.md` s. 4 and 6: no ads, no account, a one-time price of about A$10 to 15. Due for iOS is a paid-upfront app at A$12.99 on the AU App Store as of 24 Nov 2026, with no iOS trial; the Mac app has a 7-day trial only through Setapp (`due-kb.txt` 288-292). Due also sells an optional Annual Upgrade Pass and a tip jar (`DO-RESEARCH.md` s. 1.2). Those are later, not this PR.

Play cannot copy Due's paid-upfront listing in the same way: this is a free download with a one-time non-consumable in-app product through the Google Play Billing Library. The library version is **9.1.0** (`com.android.billingclient:billing:9.1.0` and `billing-ktx`), which is the current release as of 18 Jun 2026 (release notes and the integrate guide, accessed 25 Nov 2026). Add it in `gradle/libs.versions.toml` the same way as the other libraries. The Billing Library manifest already merges `com.android.vending.BILLING`; do not hand-declare a second copy unless merge forces it.

**Placeholder product.** The product id is the constant `do_unlock`. Tim will create the real Play Console product later, and may rename it then. The code never hard-codes a price string. It calls `queryProductDetailsAsync` for `do_unlock` (`ProductType.INAPP`) and shows the formatted price Play returns. If Play is unreachable, show "Price unavailable" and keep Buy disabled until the details arrive, with a retry on resume. Restore stays available offline against the cache (below).

**Free versus paid, recommendation (Tim decides).** Due has no free iOS tier and no iOS trial, so there is no Due free/paid split to copy. Do's own MoSCoW Must is "usable before paying" (`DO-RESEARCH.md` s. 4). A time-limited trial is a poor fit: Due does not offer one on iOS, and nag reviewers already complain about a "mandatory free trial that will auto renew" (`DO-RESEARCH.md` s. 2.2). A one-time product cannot auto-renew, but a clock that then freezes the app is still a hard cut-off of something the user has started to rely on.

Recommended default, assumed, for Jim to build to until Tim walks it back: **the full app is free for up to 5 reminders that are not done. Unlocking removes the cap.** All shipping features (nag, repeats, natural-language add, alert levels, Quick Times, health check, export and backup) work in the free tier. Hitting Save or `.debug.ADD` on a sixth active reminder does not create it; it opens the unlock screen and, for the debug hook, logs `DoAlarm unlock blocked active=<n> cap=5`. Existing reminders, including any that arrived through import and pushed the count above 5, keep firing and can be edited, snoozed, completed or exported. The user can also mark one done and add another without paying. Done reminders do not count toward the cap. The number 5 is a Gareth call, not a Due fact.

**Purchase flow.** Settings, near the top, gets an "Unlock Do" row (Due puts Upgrade Pass and Become a Supporter at the top of Settings, `email1-image3.jpg`). Locked: the row shows the Play price when known, and opens the unlock screen. Unlocked: the row reads "Do is unlocked" and still opens a short status screen with Restore purchases. The unlock screen shows the price, a short plain pitch (no invented Due slogans; assumed copy: "Unlock Do for a one-time purchase. No ads, no account, and no subscription."), Buy, and Restore purchases. Buy calls `launchBillingFlow`. On `PURCHASED`, grant entitlement, tell the user it succeeded, then acknowledge. On user-cancel, leave the screen as it is, with no error toast. On a Billing Library error, show a plain failure and leave the user locked.

**Acknowledgement.** Non-consumable purchases must be acknowledged within three days of entering `PURCHASED`, or Google Play refunds the purchase and revokes entitlement (Billing Library 2.0 release notes, still in the 9.x integrate guide and the one-time lifecycle, accessed 25 Nov 2026). Acknowledge only when `purchaseState` is `PURCHASED` and `isAcknowledged` is false, after entitlement is granted, through `BillingClient.acknowledgePurchase()`. Do not acknowledge a `PENDING` purchase. There is no Do backend in this PR, so acknowledgement is client-side, as the integrate guide allows for a client-only app.

**Pending purchases.** One-time products must enable pending transactions when building the `BillingClient` (integrate guide: use `enablePendingPurchases` with the current 9.1.0 `PendingPurchasesParams` API for one-time products). A `PENDING` purchase does not grant entitlement. Show a plain "Payment pending" state on the unlock screen. When the purchase later becomes `PURCHASED` (listener, or `queryPurchasesAsync` on start and on every `MainActivity` resume), grant, notify and acknowledge. If a pending purchase is cancelled or declined, drop the pending state and stay locked.

**Restore.** Restore purchases, and the start/resume query, call `queryPurchasesAsync` for `ProductType.INAPP`. If `do_unlock` is returned in `PURCHASED`, grant (or keep) entitlement and acknowledge if needed. This is how a reinstall, a new phone on the same Play account, or a purchase that finished while the app was closed, comes back. A "Restore purchases" control is always visible on the unlock or status screen, even when already unlocked.

**Offline entitlement cache.** Persist a small entitlement record in credential-protected storage (same unlock rule as `SettingsStore`): unlocked or not, pending or not, the last time Play was queried, and whether the last grant came from Play or from the debug fake. Do not write a purchase token into the JSON backup file. On a cold start, apply the cache immediately so an already-unlocked user is not locked out while offline, then refresh from Play when a connection is available. If Play later says the product is no longer owned, revoke entitlement (see refunds). If Play cannot be reached, keep the cache. A cache that says locked, with no network, stays locked.

**Refunds and revocation.** Due cannot issue refunds itself; they go through the store (Due help "How do I get a refund?", `due-kb.txt` 75-76). Do the same: no in-app refund button. When `queryPurchasesAsync` no longer returns a `PURCHASED` `do_unlock` after a successful Play query, clear the cache and return to the free cap. Existing reminders stay put and keep firing. New adds are blocked once the active count is at or above the cap. Do not delete user data because a refund came in.

**Debug-only fake billing.** Automated tests and the phone runner must never talk to Play and must never make a real purchase. Do not add a Gradle product flavour for this: it would multiply the existing `exactAlarmMode` variants. Follow the DebugReceiver pattern instead. An `UnlockBilling` interface has a release implementation that calls Play, and a debug implementation (`FakeUnlockBilling`) that never starts a billing connection. Release builds must not contain the fake, and `checkAlarmManifests` (or a sibling check in that task) must assert that. Debug builds default to **unlocked** through the fake, so a Spec 7 debug APK does not break the Spec 1 to 6 runners. Cap and purchase-state tests lock or pending-set the fake first. No licence-tester or real-card path is part of this PR.

### B. Local backup

**What Due actually shows and documents.** The Tools menu, in order, is Export, Print, Backup, then Sync with the subtitle "Reminders Not Backed Up", then Help (`email2-image4.jpg`). Due stores data as a gzip JSON file with the extension `.duedb` (article 360004202572). Backup > Current, then share, is how a user gets a copy. Due also "automatically keeps a local backup of your database every time you return to the Home Screen"; those in-app copies are deleted if the app is removed, and Restore is Menu > Backup, then the latest backup (article 215118506). From 21.3, a `.duedb` can be opened in Due, previewed and restored, and shared through other apps. From 25.3, individual reminders can be restored from a backup. Due's visible "Reminders Not Backed Up" text sits under Sync, which is iCloud or Dropbox (`DO-RESEARCH.md` s. 1.3). Do has no cloud account and is not building Sync in this PR, so the same warning is attached to a user-controlled file backup instead. That attachment is assumed.

**Tools menu.** On the Spec 6 list menu, add a Tools group with **Export** and **Backup**, in that order, copied from Due. Do not add Print or Sync rows in this PR (they would be dead ends). Help stays out unless it already exists. Australian English on every new string.

**Export.** Export opens the Storage Access Framework create-document picker (`ACTION_CREATE_DOCUMENT`, type `application/json`) so the user chooses the location, including Google Drive through the picker. Suggested name: `do-backup-YYYYMMDD-HHmm.json`, in the device local time, 24-hour digits, no spaces. The file is UTF-8 JSON, not gzip (Due's `.duedb` is gzip JSON; Do does not claim `.duedb` compatibility, and a plain `.json` file is easier to inspect). A share sheet alone is not enough, because the user would not be able to pick Drive as a folder the same way.

**Backup screen.** Backup opens a screen with: the last successful export or auto-backup time (or "Not backed up yet"); **Backup now**; **Choose backup folder**; **Restore from file**; and a short explanation that Auto Backup to Google Drive (the system one) is separate, and that this folder is the copy the user can see. "Choose backup folder" uses `ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT_TREE` and `takePersistableUriPermission` for read and write. "Backup now" writes the same JSON into that folder when one is set, or falls through to the Export picker if not. "Restore from file" uses `ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT` and accepts `application/json` plus `*/*`, so a Drive or Downloads file can be picked.

**Schema.** Version the file. Schema 1, assumed (Due's `.duedb` schema is not published in the sources above):

```
{
  "schemaVersion": 1,
  "appId": "com.evansappstudio.doapp",
  "appVersionName": "...",
  "exportedAt": <epoch ms>,
  "settings": { ... },
  "reminders": [ { ... }, ... ]
}
```

`settings` includes the portable SettingsStore keys: default nag, the three snooze options, the twelve Quick Times slots and postpone mode, week start, parse-dates, default alert level, and repeat-prompt mode. It excludes notification-asked flags, alert-explanation flags, the Play entitlement cache, and any purchase token. Each reminder includes a stable `uuid` plus the current `Reminder` fields (`title`, `dueAt`, `nagMinutes`, `isDone`, `doneAt`, `createdAt`, the repeat columns, `patternDueAt`, `alertLevel`). Add a Room v4 migration that gives every existing row a new random UUID and every new row one at insert. Local numeric `id` values are not portable and are not required in the file; on import, match by `uuid`. Reject a file with a `schemaVersion` greater than the app supports, or with a missing or foreign `appId`, with a plain error, and do not touch the database. Older supported versions (none yet, only 1) would be migrated on read. A corrupt or truncated file is the same: error, no change.

**Restore: preview, merge or replace, and selective restore.** After a file is read and validated, show a preview: count of reminders, exported-at time, and the reminder titles. Offer **Replace all**, **Merge**, and the Due 25.3 option to pick individual reminders to bring in (that path is a merge of the selection). Replace all asks for a confirmation that names how many current reminders will be deleted, then replaces settings and reminders in one transaction, rebuilds the mirror, and calls `scheduler.rescheduleAll()`. Merge upserts by `uuid` (imported fields win) and inserts unknown uuids with new local ids, then reschedules the affected rows; it does not delete reminders that are only on the device, and it does not wipe settings unless a later prompt says so (assumed: merge is reminders-only, replace is reminders and portable settings). If the user is still in the free tier and a replace or merge would leave more than 5 active reminders, allow it (data must not be refused), keep those reminders firing, and block further adds until unlock.

**Optional periodic auto-backup.** If a backup folder is set, write a new file when the app goes to the background, or after a reminder or settings change, once at least 24 hours have passed since the last successful auto-backup. The 24-hour gap is a Gareth call (Android Auto Backup itself waits at least 24 hours, Auto Backup docs). Use the existing `AppContainer` scope, not a new exact-alarm. WorkManager is not required in this PR. A failed write (lost URI permission, full disk, or a missing folder) does not crash the app; it leaves the health row as a Warning and keeps the last successful timestamp if there was one. Auto-backup files use the same suggested name pattern, and old auto-backup files in that folder are left in place (no silent deletion). Turning the folder off, or clearing the URI, stops automatic writes but does not delete files already written.

**Android Auto Backup.** The manifest already has `android:allowBackup="true"` and no custom rules (PR #6 `AndroidManifest.xml`). Keep Auto Backup on. Add both rule files, because the app targets API 36 and must still support Android 11 and lower (Auto Backup docs): `android:fullBackupContent` for API 30 and lower, and `android:dataExtractionRules` for API 31 and higher, with matching `<cloud-backup>` and `<device-transfer>` sections. Include the Room database `do.db` (database domain) and the `do_settings` shared preferences. Exclude caches. Cache, code-cache and no-backup directories are already excluded by the system; do not store the only copy of reminders there. The device-protected `ReminderMirror` and health late-fire records can be excluded: after a restore, `AppContainer.start` already reschedules from Room once the user is unlocked. Include the entitlement cache so a restored phone stays unlocked while offline, then refresh from Play. Auto Backup is capped at 25 MB per app and is not readable by the user (Auto Backup docs). Do not treat a system Auto Backup as clearing the "Reminders Not Backed Up" warning.

**"Reminders Not Backed Up" health row.** Spec 6 call 11 left this out so this spec could share the badge. Add a health item after the existing ones, id `backup`, title "Reminders Not Backed Up" when unhealthy (Due's wording) and "Backup" when OK. Warning, and it lights the badge, when there has never been a successful Export, Backup now, or auto-backup to a user-picked location, or when the last attempt failed and there is no later success. OK once one of those has succeeded; the explanation then includes the last success time in the device locale. Fix opens the Backup screen. Status Check is not used here. Unit-test the new evaluator branch. `DUMP_HEALTH` gains `backup=OK` or `backup=WARN`.

## 6. Risks
- **Play Payments policy.** In-app unlock of app functionality on a Play-distributed app must use Google Play's billing system (Payments policy, accessed 25 Nov 2026). This PR uses only Play Billing in release. There must be no link, copy or button that sends the user to another way to pay. Alternative-payment or sideload pricing is out of scope.
- **Unacknowledged purchases are refunded after three days.** A missed `acknowledgePurchase()` looks like a successful buy that then vanishes. The grant-then-acknowledge path, plus a resume query, is the mitigation. This cannot be fully proved on the phone in this PR without a licence tester, so unit tests carry that burden and AT-4 stays a UI check.
- **Pending and revoked purchases.** Granting on `PENDING`, or keeping entitlement after a refund, would be a policy and trust defect. The fake covers the state machine; real Play pending cards stay for a later licence-tester pass that Tim would have to approve, because it needs Play Console setup.
- **Offline cache versus a refund.** If Play cannot be reached, a refunded user could stay unlocked until the next successful query. That is the documented trade-off of a client-only cache. Do not invent a time-to-live that Play does not require; do refresh on every resume.
- **SAF permission loss.** A user-picked Drive folder can lose its persistable URI after reboot, app reinstall or a Drive-side change. That must surface as a backup Warning, not a crash, and Fix must let them pick again.
- **Auto Backup is invisible and capped at 25 MB.** It can restore a reinstall, but the user cannot open the file, and a dataset over 25 MB is skipped (`onQuotaExceeded`). The user-visible Export / folder backup is the real safety net, which is why it alone clears the warning.
- **Room v4 on a test phone.** Installing this APK over a future higher database version will fail to open Room. The runner uninstalls first in that case. Mark should not rely on install `-r` alone.
- **Debug default-unlocked.** A sideloaded debug build will not show the cap unless the fake is locked. That is deliberate so older runners keep working. The release build must start locked until Play says otherwise; the manifest and fake-absence checks are what catch a mistake.
